Research Scientist

Persona Identities

San Francisco, CA, USA

I’m a Research Scientist at Persona Identities, where I build machine-learning systems to fight identity fraud. Before joining industry, I spent a decade as a researcher at UCLA, studying the physics of Earth’s radiation belts and magnetosphere using spacecraft data and large-scale data-driven models.

My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, plasma physics, and AI for real-world systems. I have led NASA- and NSF-funded projects developing neural-network models of the near-Earth space environment, and I have published in journals such as Nature Communications, Geophysical Research Letters, and JGR: Space Physics. More recently, I’ve been working on layout-aware representation learning for open-set fraud detection (CVPR/AI4RWC 2026).

I earned my Ph.D. in Space Physics from Peking University in 2015, with a visiting year at UCLA under Prof. Richard Thorne, and a B.S. in Applied Physics from Xi’an Jiaotong University.

Outside of research, I serve on the boards of several Chinese-American scientific and alumni organizations, chairing scholarship programs that support students in STEM.
